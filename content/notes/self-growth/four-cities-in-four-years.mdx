New York. Dallas. Paris. Seattle.

I was fortunate to have the opportunity to live in four cities over the past
four years of my undergraduate career.

The most valuable lessons I took from it are these:

- **It's going to hurt. Let it:** Before I moved to Paris, I assumed that I
  would transition quite easily into my new French lifestyle. I was going to
  peruse Parisian bakeries all day and spend my time exploring the Eiffel Tower
  and the Pantheon, all with a smile on my face. I supposed that I would face
  very few challenges and whichever ones I did face, I would take them in
  stride. As you can presume, the reality was quite different from that. On my
  first day, I was already searching up how to go back. I wasn't used to the
  tiny quarters that are typical of European living; I could find maybe two
  vegetarian dishes on the menus of most restaurants. I felt horrible knowing
  that my circumstances were so different from how I imagined them to be. What
  I've now realized is that moving cities is a big change, and like any change,
  there _will_ be growing pains involved. It's important not to add to the
  resentment by focusing on the gap between how things are and how you thought
  they would be.
- **Open yourself up to new ways of doing things:** Growing up in suburban New
  Jersey, I was used to cars as my primary mode of transportation. When I moved
  to NYC, I had to learn how to use the subway system, and it enabled me to get
  around much more quickly and affordably. I carried this skill with me to Paris
  and Seattle, where I was easily able to maneuver the cities due to my past
  experience. In Dallas, my roommate introduced me to micheladas and Mexican
  culture, and I shared Indian traditions back; by staying open to new foods and
  experiences, my worldview expanded exponentially. In Paris, I learned to dress
  differently, navigate a city in a language I didn't speak, and find vegetarian
  food in a meat-heavy culture. Each city asked me to abandon some part of my
  default way of living and replace it with something that actually fit where I
  was. By doing so, I grew and learned so much more than if I would have just
  recreated my old life in a new place.
- **Be okay with being alone: ** One of the obvious but most easily forgotten
  parts of moving to a new city is that you won't know many people. This, as a
  result, will require you to do many things by yourself. The cliché advice of
  "learning to love being alone" seems a bit patronizing in these moments when
  you desperately wish someone would be next to you. The truth is, you don't
  have to _love_ being by yourself, but you do have to _be okay_ with it, or you
  won't get anything done. If I had waited for someone to do things with, I
  never would have seen the cliffs of Normandy, or the Sacré-Coeur, or
  Bainbridge Island – which were all experienes that inexplicably changed me, by
  the way.
- **Let people surprise you: ** In a contradictory vein, don't be so focused on
  being independent that you completely abandon the idea of making new friends
  as well. On my 21st birthday, I had just moved to Paris, completely certain
  that no one knew of my special day. Somehow, two acquaintances (who are now
  good friends) surprised me with a cake and a spontaneous trip to Copenhagen;
  what started as an ordinary day became one of the most special moments of my
  life. So, while you should be self-sufficient, stay open to the idea of
  beautiful surprises.
