i saw a tiktok recently about how the reason i can't let go of the past is
because i don't believe another great thing will happen again.

which makes sense. my sample size of truly great things happening is 1 (one).
and when your sample size is one, that one thing becomes the entire dataset. it
becomes proof that life peaked, that you already used up your allocation, that
the universe dealt you one good hand and now you're just playing out the rest of
the game with whatever's left. you hold the memory so tightly not because you're
sentimental but because you're terrified it's all there is.

but in order to truly move on and be excited and present in my life, i need to
convince myself that not only will another great thing happen, but multiple
will, and they will be even better than what i've experienced so far.

the question is: why don't i believe that?

i've been sitting with this, and i think there are three reasons. not one.
three, tangled up together, and depending on the day, a different one is
loudest.

the pressure.

my mindset shapes my reality. if i don't believe another great thing will
happen, then it won't. and the moment i understood this, instead of feeling
liberated, i felt a wave of anxiety so intense it almost knocked me over.
because it means i need to fix my entire orientation toward the future right
now. today. immediately. or i'm sabotaging myself with every passing second of
doubt.

which is, of course, its own trap. the belief that i need to be optimistic right
now in order to not ruin my life is maybe the least optimistic thought i've ever
had. the cure becomes the disease. the awareness that your mindset matters
becomes a new thing to fail at.

the worthiness.

this one is harder to say out loud.

i don't think i'm the kind of person great things happen to on purpose. the
first time felt accidental, like grace that wandered into the wrong room. not
something i summoned or earned or could ever reproduce. just something that
happened near me, once, for reasons i still don't fully understand.

and if it was an accident, then hoping for a second time isn't optimism. it's
delusion. that's how the logic goes on a bad day: you are not the type. stop
waiting for something that was never meant for you.

i notice this most when i lose people. it happens to everyone. i know this
intellectually. when someone else drifts apart from a friend, when someone else
has a falling out, i think: that's just life. people come and go. it doesn't
mean anything about who you are. but when it happens to me, i can't extend
myself that same grace. when i lose someone, it becomes evidence. proof that
something in me is fundamentally wrong, that they saw it, and that eventually
everyone will. every lost friendship becomes a verdict on my character rather
than a natural part of being alive.

and that double standard is its own kind of cruelty. i offer the world a
compassion i refuse to turn inward.

the protection.

even if i could believe it, even if i could override the pressure and the
unworthiness, there's a third thing: i know that to let great things in, i have
to be visible. and being visible is where the danger lives.

i have to put myself out there to allow more great things to happen. but putting
myself out there increases the probability of making mistakes. and mistakes
terrify me. not because i made them, but because of what comes after.

what if people don't like me? what if they store it away, the clumsy thing i
said, the wrong read on a room, and use it against me later? what if someone who
witnessed my worst moment is sitting on it quietly, and one day it resurfaces to
close a door i didn't even know i was walking toward?

i know how this sounds. i'm describing a world where every social interaction is
a liability and every person is a potential witness for the prosecution. and
that's not what i believe on a good day. but on a bad day, the logic feels
airtight. i tried my best to be good and kind and helpful, and some people still
didn't like me. and that's the part i can't metabolize. not the mistakes
themselves, but the fact that they didn't come with a receipt i could return
them with.

so i stay inside. not always physically, but psychologically. i keep the radius
small. i don't put the thing out there, don't say the thing, don't go to the
thing. because if i'm not seen, i can't be hurt. and if i can't be hurt, i can't
lose anything else.

it is a very quiet way to disappear.

i think this is also where nazar lives in me.

for those unfamiliar: nazar is the evil eye. in south asian (and many other)
cultures, it's the belief that being seen, especially being seen doing well,
attracts harm. not metaphorically. really. your grandmother puts a black dot
behind your ear before you leave the house. you don't announce good news too
loudly. you protect yourself from the gaze of others because the gaze itself
carries weight.

i grew up with this. and i think it shaped something deep in the way i move
through the world, something beneath language, beneath logic. most western
self-help will tell you to be visible, be loud, manifest abundance, put yourself
out there. but i come from a tradition where visibility is the danger. where the
great thing happening is exactly what makes you a target for losing it.

so when i say i'm afraid to go outside, i'm not just being anxious. i'm carrying
a whole cosmology of protection that says: the eye is real, and it finds the
people who shine.

nazar touches all three threads, actually. it reinforces the pressure: don't let
anyone see you wanting things, or you'll jinx them. it deepens the unworthiness:
maybe you were never supposed to have it, and wanting it is hubris. and it
justifies the protection: see? being seen literally invites harm. it's the
thread that runs through the entire knot, the one that makes every other thread
harder to loosen.

lately i've been noticing that all of my problems are interconnected like this.
like a tangled ball of yarn. when i deal with one of them, even a little, it
loosens the thread of another one. and then i gain more clarity on that second
thing, and i can work on it more easily, and so on. the knots aren't separate.
they're the same knot, just viewed from different angles.

i think this essay is me trying to loosen one of those threads.

here's where the yarn started to loosen for me.

i was spiraling the other day, doing the thing where anxiety takes a real fear
and escalates it step by step until it becomes absurd. it went something like:
i'll make a mistake → people will hate me → they'll tell other people → it'll
follow me → i'll get passed up for opportunities → i'll get sued → i'll go to
jail.

jail. i arrived at jail.

and then i stopped and went, "wait. jail? for saying the wrong thing at a
dinner? that's so extra."

and i laughed. not because the fear wasn't real, but because i caught the
machinery in the act. i watched my brain take "i was awkward" and, in about four
seconds, construct an airtight legal case for why my life is over. and the thing
about catching yourself mid-spiral is that suddenly the original fear looks
smaller too. the catastrophe at the end of the chain was absurd, which means
every link leading to it was less solid than it felt.

and then something quieter occurred to me.

even the bad versions. even the ones that aren't jail but are real and painful.
getting passed up for something i wanted. someone thinking less of me. a door
closing because of something i said years ago. even those are survivable.

not fun. not fair. but survivable.

and that changes the entire calculation. not just for the protection, but for
all three threads at once.

the pressure to be optimistic right now? it loosens if i know i can survive
being wrong. the unworthiness? it's hard to keep arguing "you're not the type"
to someone who keeps getting back up. and the protection, the whole logic of
staying inside, it starts to collapse once the worst case stops being
unsurvivable.

i think i was never really asking "will another great thing happen?" i was
asking something underneath that, something i couldn't quite look at directly:
can i survive the cost of letting it?

the answer to the first question requires a faith i don't have yet. the answer
to the second is just: yes. i've already survived things. i'm still here. i will
still be here.

the evil eye might land. people might store my mistakes. i might say the wrong
thing at the wrong time to the wrong person. someone might leave me again and i
might, again, take it as proof that i am broken. all of that can be true, and i
can still be standing on the other side of it.

i think the real danger was never being seen. the real danger was staying so
small that my sample size never grew past one. because that is the only scenario
where i am guaranteed the thing i'm most afraid of: a life where nothing great
happens again. not because the universe ran out of great things, but because i
never gave it the chance to deal another hand.

i don't have this figured out. i am still half inside. but the thread is a
little looser now than when i started writing this.

maybe that's enough for today.
