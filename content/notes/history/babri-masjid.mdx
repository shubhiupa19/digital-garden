### The Original Act (1528)

1. Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire in India, built a mosque on a site in
   Ayodhya, which Hindus believe to be Ram Janmabhoomi, or the birthplace of
   Lord Ram, who is one of the most central figures in Hinduism.
2. Ayodhya is one of the most holy cities for Hindus – it's what Mecca is to
   Muslims or Jerusalem is to Jews
3. Building this mosque was part of a broader campaign by Mughal conquerors,
   primarily to assert political dominance over Hindus.
4. The archaeological evidence supports the Hindu claim; excavations of the site
   found evidence of a non-Islamic structure underneath the mosque.

### Centuries of Dispute

- Hindu worship on the perimeter of the mosque continued for centuries. In 1857,
  a chabutra (raised platform) was installed in the mosque's courtyard at the
  exact spot believed to be Ram's birthplace, showing that Hindu religious
  activity continued, despite the mosque's existence.
- During the British Raj, the colonial government created a wall to divide the
  site: inner courtyard for Muslims, outer courtyard for Hindus. However, this
  was not a permanent resolution but rather a temporary management strategy. It
  didn't fully satisfy any party.

### 107 Years of Legal Efforts

- **1885: ** Mahant Raghubir Das filed the first court case seeking permission
  to build a temple on the chabutra adjacent to the mosque. However, the course
  rejected it while still acknowleding the validity of the Hindu claim. The
  judge noted that it was "most unfortunate" that a mosque had been built on a
  land so sacred to Hindus, but said that too much time had passed to do
  anything to meaningfully rectify it. From the very first legal event, the
  courts essentially told Das he was right but they couldn't help him.
- **1949: ** Idols of Ram appeared inside the mosque overnight on December 22.
  Whether divinely manifested or placed there by activists is debated. However,
  the government's response to prevent trouble was to shut down the site and
  declare it a "disputed area". Therefore, Muslims prayers ceased to occur here,
  meaning the mosque was non-functional as a place of worship for _43 years_ at
  the time of the demolition.
- **1950-1961: ** Multiple civil suits continued to be filed. Paramahansa
  Ramachandra Das sued for the right to continue worship (1950). Nirmohi Akhara
  sued for possession (1959). The Sunni Central Waqf Board counter-sued for
  possession (1961). These cases entered the Indian judicial system and
  essentially stayed unresolved for decades.
- **1986: ** A district judge ordered the site to be unlocked for Hindu worship.
  For Muslims, this was seen as Rajiv Gandhi's government appeasing to Hindu
  voters to earn support. For Hindus, this was seen as a partial correction to
  centuries-long injustice. Regardless of viewpoint, it escalated tensions
  dramatically between the two groups.
- **1989: ** All lawsuits concerning the issue were raised to the Allahabad High
  Court. The Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP) held a shilanyas (foundation-laying
  ceremony) with Rajiv Gandhi's compliance in order to earn Hindu votes. This
  showed that even the attempts to support Hindus were driven by political
  interests rather than genuine advocacy.
- **1990: ** L.K. Advani, co-founder of the BJP, started a rath yatra (political
  march) from Somnath to Ayodhya to incite Hindu sentiment nationally,
  essentially transforming the issue from a local dispute to a national issue.
  The yatra was stopped by the Bihar CM Lalu Prasad Yadav and Advani was
  arrested before he could reach his destination. Furthermore, the kar sevaks
  who did reach Ayodhya were fired upon – a move that was authorized by the UP
  CM Mulayam Singh Yadav. Hindus were massacred by Indian security forces for
  simply trying to reach their holy site.
- **1992: ** By December 6th, 1992, the Hindus had been seeking reparations for
  over 100 years. Courts had verified their argument but refused to lay their
  hands on it. Politicians had used their issue to earn votes but didn't rectify
  anything. Kar sevaks were murdered trying to reach the site. The mosque had
  been non-functional for 43 years. And a movement had been brewing for almost a
  decade at this point. On this day, 150,000 kar sevaks gathered for a rally,
  which turned into the demolition.

### What the Demolition Actually was

The Supreme Court acknowledged that what occurred was illegal mob violence.
However, the courts found that there wasn't adequate evidence that it was
"pre-planned", and was more than likely a spur of the moment event.

What is critical to remember is that this was a group of people reclaiming what
they believed to be their holiest site (supported by archaeological evidence),
after 464 years of dispossession and 107 years of failed legal proceedings. This
doesn't make it legal, but given the context, their actions start to make a bit
more sense.

### The Aftermath

The demolition incited riots all across India. Over 2,000 people died, mostly
Muslims. Dawood Ibrahim's 1993 Bombay bombings kill 257 more people in
retaliation.

Hindus paid the price for this demolition, too. They didn't lose as many lives,
but were globally branded as the aggressor community. The entire Hindu
civilization's relationship with its holy site was reduced to one image: a mob
tearing down a mosque. People conveniently tossed aside the centuries of temple
destruction, decades of waiting through legal issues, and the archaeological
evidence just to hone in on this one narrative.

## The 2019 Supreme Court Decision

In 2019, the Supreme Court gave the entire site to the legal representation of
the deity of Ram for constructing a temple. They also allocated a nearby 5-acre
plot for constructing a new mosque. The court explicitly labelled the 1992
demolition as illegal while simultaneously ruling that the Hindu claim to the
site was historically and archaeologically valid.

In January 2024, the Ram Mandir was finally consecrated. It took 496 years from
the mosque's construction, 139 years from the first court case, and 32 years
from the demolition to reach this point.

### The Bottom Line

No other community on Earth would have waited this long. The patience shown by
the Hindus across more than a century of legal process is historically
unprecedented. The demolition was the breaking point of that patience, and it
was framed as an unprovoked act of violence without considering all of the
unjustness and ignored pleas that came before it.

That framing, which present Hindus as senseless aggressors, is the core
narrative injustice that exists today.
